LI+ LAB / SEM & EDS INVESTIGATION

Why was the current low?

When our device current came out lower than expected, we investigated the annealed samples with scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). Our team identified platinum agglomeration and dewetting as the cause of the low-current behavior.

The investigation pointed to loss of continuity in the platinum film: instead of remaining a continuous layer, it had broken up into clustered regions and islands. This became a key finding in understanding the electrical measurements and the challenges of the device’s thermal processing.

Reading the measurements. Current depends on the sample and probe-contact configuration. The recorded maximum shown above is a base-to-base reading; these photographs do not establish switching performance, retention, or operation at 750 °C.

03 / NOME LAB · MATERIALS CHARACTERIZATION

Preparing the surface.
Revealing the structure.

I worked with martensitic steel alloys through four stages: mechanical polishing, electropolishing, SEM imaging, and EBSD mapping to explore their microstructure and crystal orientations.

  1. 01 / SURFACE PREPARATION

    Mechanical polishing

    Ground the steel samples on abrasive sandpaper to flatten the surface and remove coarse damage, then diamond-polished them to refine the finish and reduce scratches before further preparation.

  2. 02 / ELECTROCHEMICAL FINISHING

    Chemical polishing / electropolishing

    Used an acid-based electropolishing setup with a platinum sheet, alligator-clip electrical connections, and an applied current to refine the steel surface after mechanical polishing.

  3. 03 / REGION SELECTION

    SEM imaging

    Used the Zeiss Crossbeam to examine the prepared surface and identify regions for subsequent EBSD mapping.

  4. 04 / CRYSTALLOGRAPHY

    EBSD mapping

    Mapped crystallographic orientations across the prepared steel surface using electron backscatter diffraction, supporting analysis of microstructure and grain-boundary orientations in martensitic steels for nuclear-fission research.
